# Example wines

These bottles are fictional. They show how the notes talk about measurements. They do not include a taster's quality score.

## North cellar sample A

Sample A is a red wine. The lab sheet lists residual sugar at 2.1 grams per litre, so the notes call it dry. Alcohol is 9.4 percent by volume. Volatile acidity is 0.70 grams per litre, which the measurement notes treat as high. The sheet does not give a quality score.

## South cellar sample B

Sample B is a white wine. Residual sugar is 12.6 grams per litre, so the notes call it sweet. Alcohol is 11.2 percent by volume. Citric acid is 0.49 grams per litre. The sheet does not give a quality score.

## How to read a sheet

Read the measurement, then use the measurement notes to say what the number refers to. A sweet sample and a dry sample can both be well or poorly regarded by tasters. Sweetness is residual sugar. Regard is the quality score. The notes keep those apart.
